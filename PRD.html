1. Overview

Ofrenda is a contest entry for Hacktoberfest Week 4, "Ghost in the Machine." It takes an abandoned open-source project and does two things with it:

  1. Forecasts when it stopped answering people, not when it stopped committing — producing two nested probability curves whose gap is the "ghosting band."
  2. Interviews it. A local open-weight model speaks as the project, citing its own issues and its deprecation notice, and says "the archive is silent on that" when it has nothing.

The corpse is request/request: the deprecated Node.js HTTP client. Its death is pinned to three dated events (O-11 settled against primary sources — logged §20): maintenance mode declared 2019-03-30 in issue #3142, which is still open (437 comments, ~3k +1s) and promises "releases will be fully automated, any merge into master will be published" — so post-2019 releases are bot-authored, and the death announcement is itself an unanswered thread the ghost can cite. npm deprecation 2020-02-11 (registry time field, v2.88.2). README rewritten as fully deprecated ~June 2020 (to be pinned via commit history at probe time). The repo still received pushes in 2024 and was never archived. Expected-shape anchor: 2019-03-30. It is held out of training, so its forecast is entirely out-of-sample.

Nothing has been built. No data has been pulled. The probe has not run.

2. Problem and thesis

Thesis. A project dies years before its last commit. Maintainers stop answering strangers long before they stop pushing code, and that gap is the difference between abandoned and dead.

The last-commit date is the common proxy for "is this maintained?" and it arrives late. Ofrenda tests whether a social signal — a maintainer answering an outsider — gives earlier warning than code activity does.

Why it is testable. There is no "death date" field in any dataset, and death is a judgement, not an observation. So the project predicts something observable instead: whether a maintainer answered an issue in the 18 months after a reference date T.

The definition that matters. A comment counts only when author_association is OWNER, MEMBER or COLLABORATOR, the author is not a bot, and the commenter is not the person who opened that issue. Reviewing a contributor's pull request is not answering a stranger. That distinction is the thesis, and it must survive into the code.

The label is a proxy for social death, not death. The write-up must say this in those words.

3. Goals and non-goals

Goals

  • Submit a complete, rule-compliant entry before the deadline.
  • Test one claim with a pre-registered analysis: social signals are not materially worse than code signals at predicting maintainer silence.
  • Produce calibrated probabilities with intervals, never bare point estimates.
  • Ship an interview with request/request that is grounded in its own record.
  • Enter all 19 available prize categories (all except Arduino) with genuinely meaningful use.
  • Publish a write-up that states its limitations before a judge finds them.

Non-goals

  • Reviving the project. Revival is an epilogue: a proposed PR, never claimed to work.
  • Predicting a death date. TabPFN has nothing to condition on at n = 1.
  • Naming or puppeting a maintainer. The ghost speaks as the project, never as a person.
  • Winning more than one category. The contest allows one win per challenge.
  • Critiquing the people who burned out maintaining request. Register: elegy, not autopsy.
  • Best-in-class statistical novelty. Two arms and one honest test beats four arms and no time.

The primary test, stated correctly

This is the most important correction in this document, and it changes the conclusion you are allowed to draw.

The thesis predicts parity: if social signals lead, then at a fixed T the social arm should predict the next 18 months at least as well as the code arm, because the code signal has not moved yet. A superiority test would report that prediction as a failure.

H0: AUC_A − AUC_B ≥ 0.07     (social is materially worse)
H1: AUC_A − AUC_B < 0.07     (social is not materially worse)
One-sided α = 0.05
Non-inferiority declared if the upper bound of the one-sided CI
on (AUC_A − AUC_B) falls below 0.07.

ΔAUC 0.07 is the non-inferiority margin, not a success threshold. Superiority is reported descriptively and never used as the pass/fail criterion.

If the result is inconclusive — CI straddling the margin — that is reported as inconclusive. It is not reframed as a win.

5. Scope

Two artifacts, one corpse.

Forecast. Two nested probabilities per reference date across a nine-point grid, a ghosting band with a bootstrap ribbon, and a lead-time analysis. Descriptive, not decorative.

Interview. A local Gemma model, retrieval over the corpse's cached archive, strict citation-or-silence, plus an altar page (final commit, oldest unanswered issue, the stale line in the README, the forecast curve).

19 integrations, six load-bearing and thirteen thin but real. See §7.

Out of scope: revival beyond a proposed diff; any UI beyond the altar page; any model training beyond TabPFN's in-context fitting and one Tinker fine-tune.

Endpoints and cost

One endpoint cannot produce a valid label. /issues/comments also returns comments on pull requests, so without a join the label degrades to "responded to anything" — which is not the thesis.

  • /issues?state=all&since= → issue author, created_at, pull_request filter
  • /issues/comments?since= → comment author, author_association, timestamp

≈ 18,000 calls for 300 repos ≈ 3.5–4 hours at 5,000/hr. REST, not GraphQL: GraphQL is billed by node cost and blows the budget on heavily commented corpses.

Fetch in stages — but every stream is required. An earlier draft said "comments first — labels need only these." That is not implementable, and is corrected here (logged §20): y1 requires the issues join (PRs are identified by the pull_request key on issue objects, and each issue's opener comes from the same fetch), and y2 must also see commits and releases. No label can be computed without all four streams. Staging therefore means: fetch repo meta first and bail on degenerate or pathological corpses before paying for the remaining streams — not comments-only labels.

Store events, not features. Raw NDJSON at fetch time; labels and features are pure functions over that cache. Relabelling is then free and competing label definitions can be tested against one fetch. Aggregates bake in window choices and are expensive to retract.

The probe is rate-bound, not latency-bound

18,000 calls at 5,000/hr is 3.6 hours regardless of per-repo latency, provided concurrency saturates the budget. So the probe logs calls per repo, not wall-clock per repo. At 60 calls/repo the full pull is 3.6h; at 120 it is 7.2h and the timeline breaks. That is the number that predicts the schedule.

8. Labels and masks

At reference date T, outcome window [T, T + 18 months).

Target Equals 1 when Reported as
y1 No maintainer answered a stranger's issue in the window P(≥ GHOSTING)
y2 No human activity of any kind in the window P(≥ DEAD)

Nesting holds by construction: y2 = 1 ⇒ y1 = 1. The three states read off them are ALIVE (y1 = 0), GHOSTING (y1 = 1, y2 = 0), DEAD (y2 = 1).

Monotonicity. Fit y2 conditionally on y1 = 1 and multiply: p2 = p1 · P(y2 | y1 = 1), so p2 ≤ p1 always. Isotonic projection is a no-op check, not a correction. Report the violation rate either way.

Masks

  1. T ≥ creation + 12 months — features need history.
  2. ≥ 1 human event in [T − 12 months, T) — without this, a repo silent since 2016 yields a trivially DEAD row for every later T. That is free accuracy and a meaningless AUC. This is the single easiest way to produce a beautiful, wrong result.

Print post-mask row counts and y2 prevalence before trusting the second curve. Mask 2 removes the rows that supply most y2 = 1 cases.

9. Features and arms

Banned class

Any recency transform over an event stream the outcome window also reads. The clearest case is days_since_last_human_commit — nearly the label in disguise, which would make the thesis unfalsifiable by proving the trivial "recent ⇒ alive."

docs_vs_code_divergence is dropped: its denominator is days since the last code change, which is exactly such a transform.

Registered features

ID Arm Feature
f1 A Release-cadence decay: ratio of inter-release interval medians, recent 24mo vs prior 24mo. Requires a never_released indicator — many dead repos never released at all
f2 A Commit-interval Shannon entropy, 12mo
f3 B Open-issue backlog slope, 12mo. Human-opened only — dependabot corrupts it
f4 B Maintainer response-latency trend: median recent vs prior 12mo, answered stranger issues
f5 B Response-rate decline: share of stranger-opened issues answered, recent vs prior
f6 B Maintainer-comment volume ratio, recent vs prior 12mo
bf50 control Authors covering 50% of commits in [T−12mo, T)

The arms must be capacity-matched

A naive split gives Arm A two features and Arm B four. A non-inferiority test where one arm has twice the capacity is confounded in the direction of the hypothesis — B has more chances to capture signal, so "parity" is partly capacity.

Primary test uses two features per arm: - A₂ = {f1, f2} - B₂ = {f4, f5} — the two most thesis-relevant social signals

f3 and f6 are reported descriptively only. No new features are invented to pad Arm A.

Every fit also carries background controls: log10(stars), repo age at T, language, created-year cohort. These are matched-pair controls, not competitors to the signal under test.

Say this in the write-up

With the strongest code feature banned, feature counts differ even after matching. Report the counts beside the result, and state that part of any gap is structural.

10. Analysis plan

Nothing here may change after the first outcome is observed. Additions are permitted only if timestamped before the probe completes and logged in the change table (§20).

Split

Leave-one-repo-out over (repo, T) rows. Several reference dates per repo would otherwise leak a project's own history across the split. TabPFN is in-context, so held-out repos are simply excluded from the context set.

Comparison hierarchy — fixed-sequence gatekeeping

Not a pairwise matrix. Six tests at α = 0.05 gives ~26% family-wise error.

Order Test Rule
1. Primary, confirmatory A₂ vs B₂ non-inferiority One-sided α = 0.05, margin 0.07
2. Descriptive Superiority A₂ vs B₂, full-feature A vs B No inference claimed
3. Descriptive Per-bus-factor-quartile AUC Interaction only, no inference

B′ and C arms are dropped to fit the timeline. Consequence: the claim "the social signal survives structural controls" comes out of the write-up entirely. Registering it and not testing it would be worse.

Instruments

  • Paired DeLong for the AUC difference — the models share rows, so pairing is far more sensitive than comparing point estimates. An unpaired SE at n = 300 is ~0.045, which is why a bare 0.07 threshold proves nothing.
  • Repo-clustered bootstrap (≥1,000 resamples) as the confirmatory check. DeLong assumes independent rows; rows from one repo are not independent. If the two disagree, the bootstrap governs.
  • Brier Skill Score vs base rate (1 − Brier/Brier_ref). Raw Brier is uninterpretable.
  • Reliability diagram — Brier conflates calibration with resolution.
  • Band ribbon from the same bootstrap. The band is a difference of two probabilities, so calibration error compounds in the subtraction.

Lead-time analysis (descriptive, cheap, high value)

For each repo, find the first T at which each arm's p(DEAD) crosses 0.5, and compare that date to the actual death date. Non-inferiority shows social is as good; only lead time shows it is earlier — which is what the thesis actually claims. Costs no α and produces the chart the argument needs.

11. External validation

archived:true is GitHub's own death certificate and the only ground truth here that is not an opinion. It is also selected — owners archive what they care about — so it is a stratum, never a clean sample. request/request is permanently outside this validation set.

Test Reference date Purpose
Primary T = archive − 36 months Window closes 18 months before declaration; real uncertainty remains
Sanity T = archive − 18 months Window ends at declaration — near-tautological, boundary check only

20 of the 60 archived corpses are held out entirely. Validating on repos seen in training is in-sample and worth nothing. Archival status is a label source and must never enter the feature schema.

12. The interview

  • Local Gemma via Ollama. gemma3:12b recommended (~8 GB); 2b will not produce a convincing interview.
  • Commits act as memory, issues as arguments, docs as self-image.
  • Every answer carries a citation (issue #, commit SHA, README line) or the sentence "the archive is silent on that." No improvisation.
  • Two voices, split by artifact. Elegy in the interview display. Plain autopsy-descriptive prose in evidence and methodology sections. Mixing them makes either the story or the method unreadable.
  • The ghost speaks as the project. No individual maintainer is named, and no claim about anyone's state of mind goes in unsourced.
  • The interview is n = 1. It is a story, not evidence, and the write-up must not present it as evidence.

13. Probe — 20 repos, before the full pull

The probe exists to break the pipeline, not confirm it. A random sample tests the happy path; spend the 20 on cases that would kill the full pull.

15. Limitations — to publish, verbatim

  1. Two definitions of DEAD coexist: declared (archived) and inferred (silence). The model learns a blend.
  2. The archived stratum is selected. Owners archive what they care about; request was deprecated but never archived.
  3. Missingness correlates with age. Pre-2012 repos have unreliable issue history.
  4. Issue features are undefined where issues are disabled, and not at random.
  5. n = 300 is small for three latent states. Report intervals, never bare point estimates.
  6. The label is a proxy for social death, not death itself.
  7. The interview half is n = 1: a story, not evidence.
  8. The primary claim is non-inferiority, not superiority. Parity is the prediction.
